Sherman Bytes started as one pixel tank on a live webpage. Its tanks now drive two full board games: DOM Battlefield, a squad tactics game, and Battlefield Blitz, a free-for-all arcade brawl. Here is how one small game grew a family.

[text]
heading: One tank

Sherman Bytes puts an M4-inspired pixel tank onto the page you are reading. Letters are walls, buttons are enemy bases, and you hold the page against escalating squads.

Drive with WASD or the arrows, aim with the mouse, fire with a click or Space. On phones, a joystick drives and a round Fire button shoots.

You can dig in, too. Build cover and pillboxes, capture a base, and let it repair your armour while the next wave comes.

It was built to stay small and polite. It caps tanks at 32 and shells at 120, and Recall gives the page back when you are done.
[/text]

[text]
heading: A whole platoon

Playing Sherman, I kept wanting more tanks on my side. DOM Battlefield is that wish, played out on a full board.

You pick one of four operations, a side and a formation, then a squad of 4, 8 or 12 tanks per side. A four-slot loadout of scout, medium and heavy tanks has to fit a 12-point budget.

Then you plan. Time freezes and you give each tank an order: Attack HQ, Follow me, or Command, where you plant a flag and draw the route.

Start the battle and the plan meets the enemy. Houses, hedges and parked cars break into pixel debris. Rocks and water stay. Fences stop tanks but let shells through.

Battlefield is its own app. It borrows Sherman Bytes for the tank artwork and its cached renderer, and adds zero bytes to the Sherman bundle.
[/text]

[text]
heading: Every tank for itself

Battlefield is the slow, thinking version. I also wanted the loud one.

The Battlefield landing page already had a hero where tanks shell and flatten the headline while airstrikes land. Battlefield Blitz turned that hero into a game.

Blitz is free for all. You parachute in, grab blinking power tiles, call airstrikes and dodge mines. Die, drop back in. Most kills in three minutes wins.

There are three modes. Practice has a coach and targets that never shoot back. Solo is eight tanks, every tank for itself. Platoon puts you and three wingmen against two other platoons of four.

Blitz is also the first game built on Fizlet, the new engine. It still draws its tanks with Sherman Bytes sprites.
[/text]

[text]
heading: One embedded game, two boards

This is the pattern now. Build the tiny embedded game first, then grow its characters into board games of different styles.

Battlefield is the classic one: strategy and tactics. Blitz is the modern one: arcade action at battle-royale pace. Both play on the Battlefield site.

The tanks are the same tanks in all three. On a desktop the controls rhyme too: WASD or the arrows drive, and the mouse aims.

Next on the plan is DOM Battlefield multiplayer: two players, two pages, one battle. That is the Bolo classroom again, finally on a webpage. It is a plan, not a promise, and this blog will follow it.
[/text]
