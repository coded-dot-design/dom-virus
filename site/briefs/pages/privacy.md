DOM VIRUS has no accounts and sells nothing. Analytics runs only if you say yes, and there are no ads. The games save a few settings in your own browser, and our web host keeps standard request logs. Last updated 2026-10-31.

[text]
heading: Who we are
DOM VIRUS is a family of small open-source arcade games that play on a real webpage. It is made by Chris Cooley under the coded.design name. In this policy, "we" means the people behind that project.

This policy covers the DOM VIRUS site, the DOM Battlefield site, the Fizlet site and the games on them.
[/text]

[text]
heading: What we collect
Nothing that identifies you. There is no sign-up, no login and no form, and the site never asks for your name or email address.

The games make no network requests of their own while you play. If you accept analytics, the site (not the game) reports a few gameplay events, described under Analytics below.
[/text]

[text]
heading: What the games keep on your device
The games save a few settings in your browser's local storage: the theme, sound on or off, a best score and some game setup choices.

One short-lived value sits in session storage when you launch DOM Battlefield from the hub. It is removed as soon as the board opens.

These values stay on your device. We cannot see them. The cookies and storage page lists every one, with what it holds and why.
[/text]

[text]
heading: Hosting and request logs
The site is hosted on GitHub Pages today. Like any web host, GitHub receives the details every browser sends with a request, such as your IP address.

GitHub may log those details to run and protect its service. It handles them under its own privacy statement, not ours.

We plan to move the site to domvirus.com behind Cloudflare. From then on, Cloudflare also receives these request details, under its own privacy policy. We will update this page when that happens.
[/text]

[text]
heading: Analytics, only if you say yes
On your first visit a banner asks. Choose Accept and the site loads Google Analytics 4 and PostHog. Choose Reject, or do nothing, and neither loads: no analytics script, no request and no analytics cookie.

What they collect: the pages you view, the site that sent you, your browser, device type, screen size and rough location (country or city, worked out from your IP address), and a few gameplay events: a game starting or ending, with the game, its score and how long it ran.

Why: to count visits and see which games people play, where they stop and what to fix. We do not use it for advertising, remarketing or profiling, and we never sell it.

How long: we set Google Analytics to keep event data for 14 months. PostHog keeps event data for the retention period of our PostHog plan. The cookies they set are listed, with their lifetimes, on the cookies and storage page.

Cloudflare Web Analytics, when switched on, is cookieless and gives us totals only: which pages were read, which sites sent visitors, and broad browser, device and country figures. It does not ask for consent because it stores nothing on your device.
[/text]

[text]
heading: Who processes analytics data
Google LLC runs Google Analytics 4. Google signals and ad personalisation are off, and the consent signals for advertising (ad storage, ad user data and ad personalisation) stay denied, so the data is not used for Google ads.

PostHog Inc. runs PostHog on its US cloud. Session recording and automatic click capture are off.

Both act as our processors and may handle data in the United States. Each handles it under its own privacy policy, linked below.
[/text]

[text]
heading: What we never do
We do not sell, rent or share personal data. We run no ads, no ad pixels, no remarketing and no social media widgets.

The site loads no outside fonts. Text uses fonts already on your device.
[/text]

[text]
heading: Device features the games use
Some games use your device while you play: sound, vibration on phones, full screen, and the clipboard for the Copy code button.

All of this happens inside your browser. The Copy code button only writes to your clipboard; it never reads it. Nothing from these features leaves your device.
[/text]

[text]
heading: When you contact us
Bug reports and ideas go through GitHub issues, which are public. What you write there, and your GitHub username, can be seen by anyone and is handled under GitHub's terms.

Please keep private details out of public issues. Security reports go through GitHub's private vulnerability reporting instead, which only the maintainer can read.
[/text]

[text]
heading: Children
The games are for a general audience and ask nobody for personal information, children included.

Did a child post something personal in one of our GitHub issues? Tell us, and we will remove it where we can.
[/text]

[text]
heading: Your choices
Clear this site's data in your browser settings to remove everything the games saved. Private or incognito windows forget it when you close them.

You can also block local storage. The games still play; they just forget your settings each time.

Analytics: choose Reject in the banner, or open Cookie settings at the foot of any page and untick Analytics. The site then stops both tools on the page and deletes their cookies. If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, we treat it as a Reject until you choose otherwise.

A content blocker also stops the analytics scripts. The site works the same without them.

You can ask us to delete analytics data about you through the contact page.
[/text]

[text]
heading: Changes to this policy
If what we collect changes, we update this page and its date first. Every change is public in the site's GitHub history.

Analytics cookies are set only after you accept them in the consent banner. Nothing tracks you across other sites.
[/text]
